# History and timeline · v0.1

**Status:** All eras are **PROPOSED**. No dates, durations or causes are approved. Era names are placeholders.

Keep the timeline loose on purpose: future games may be prequels, run alongside each other, or be set centuries
apart.

| Era | What we know | Story room |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Before the Divide** | Remembered differently in each faction's archives. Magic and technology were both real; their early relationship and origins are open. | Contradictory founding myths, shared achievements, artifacts nobody can read |
| **II. Great Works** | Large infrastructure and monumental sacred places. Prosperity for some, dependence for others. Nothing says prosperity caused the collapse. | Prequels, ancient institutions, forgotten promises |
| **III. The First Failures** | Shortages, failing systems and supernatural disturbances. Records are inconsistent and explained in incompatible ways. | An investigation where every archive tells a different truth |
| **IV. The Fracture** | Divisions deepen; the three factions harden their stories and compete for resources, protection and legitimacy. Maybe a long process, not one war. | Faction origins, betrayals, families split by belief |
| **V. The Dying World** | The present emergency and the setting of the first game. The Foundry's AI pursues its salvation; the Dawnfaith wrestles with prophecy and zealotry; the Lantern Cities survive by trade and compromise. | The Foundry levels, the regional bosses, ordinary people under pressure |
| **VI. The Unwritten Future** | Not one fixed line. Character endings are ALTERNATE unless the creator promotes one. | Sequels, alternative outcomes |

**Open:** when the AI was built relative to the first failures (Seraphine's records put the first failures earlier than official history does, WC-011), and how fast the decline is
moving (Open Question 2).

## Sources inside the world

A Foundry incident log, a Dawnfaith manuscript and a Lantern trade ledger can each record the same night
differently. Each can be honest about what it saw and wrong about what it means. Record documents as **claims by
in-world sources**, never as narrator's truth.
