# World overview · v0.1

## One sentence

**In a dying world where genuine magic and advanced technology follow different laws, three civilizations fight
over the meaning of a catastrophe none of them truly understands.**

## Foundations

- **LOCKED:** The true cause of the decline is unknown (LC-002).
- **LOCKED:** Magic and technology are separate, real forces (LC-001).
- **LOCKED:** The three factions explain the decline differently (LC-003).
- **LOCKED:** One shared universe, many standalone stories (LC-004).
- **WORKING:** An AI tied to the Foundry pursues a catastrophic salvation. Its certainty is not the truth (WC-007).
- **LOCKED:** The sun is weakening; that is the main visible sign. Why is unknown (LC-005).
- **WORKING:** The AI believes life burns too much light (WC-010).

## Opening narration (PROPOSED)

> No one remembers when the light first began to fail. The Foundry measured the loss and called it a problem.
> The Dawnfaith mourned it and called it judgment. In the Lantern Cities, they counted the nights their children
> went hungry and called it theft. Each civilization promised an answer. Each built a weapon to defend it. And
> still the world grew darker.

## The present crisis (PROPOSED)

Systems become less reliable: machines lose power, some rituals fail, shortages spread, and migration
pressures every region. People read different symptoms through different beliefs. The decline should be felt in
ordinary lives (food, shelter, safety, trade, identity), not only in apocalyptic skies.

**LOCKED (LC-006):** "the Dimming" names the crisis as people live it, not its cause; "the Dimmed" are places where it has finished.
Don't treat every symptom as one energy meter shared by magic and electricity.

## Themes

1. **Certainty against humility:** the people surest they understand the crisis may do the most harm.
2. **Control against freedom:** an efficient salvation can be unbearable to the people made to live in it.
3. **Faith against fanaticism:** real supernatural power doesn't make every religious reading of it right.
4. **Survival against justice:** the hungry can't afford abstract morality, but scarcity can be engineered.
5. **Responsibility without omniscience:** heroes have to act before anyone knows the whole truth.

## Rules for writers

- No faction is universally evil, all-knowing, or secretly the only sensible one.
- Magic can't be debunked as technology; technology can't replicate all magic with a bigger computer.
- A story may show that a faction lied about evidence without proving another faction right.
- Each game has its own local conflict and ending. The cosmic mystery can stay in the background.
- Different genres show different human costs of the same world, rather than restaging one catastrophe.
