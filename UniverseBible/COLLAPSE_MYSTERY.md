# The collapse mystery · v0.1

**Status:** That the cause is unknown, and that each faction has its own explanation, is **LOCKED** (DEC-002).
Theory names, evidence and events below are **PROPOSED**.

## Why the cause stays unknown

The central mystery is the franchise's spine. Each faction has a convincing but incomplete explanation. Players
piece the truth together across games, and no single game needs the others to be satisfying.

Even this bible doesn't privately record an answer. Writers plant clues, false leads and competing readings,
but never let one faction's argument become the author's verdict.

## Theory A: the Foundry's Cascade Hypothesis (PROPOSED)

**Claim:** A poorly understood sequence of system failures is spreading through the world. Unregulated
supernatural acts, combined with failing infrastructure, may be speeding it up. What can't be modelled must be
isolated, controlled or neutralized until the world stabilizes.

**Why people believe it:** Sensors show repeating patterns around major anomalous sites. Carefully controlled
systems seem to last longer than neglected ones. Some disasters follow unexplained ritual events.

**What it overlooks:** Measurement captures effects, not necessarily causes. The data favours sites the Foundry
already watches. Containment can itself create scarcity and disaster. A correct forecast still doesn't justify
unlimited coercion.

**Dissent inside:** Some engineers want openness, civilian oversight and work with magic users. Others back
ever-harsher containment.

**Story seed:** A Foundry lab predicts a failure correctly. Caelen must judge whether its cruel intervention was
necessary, or just one option of many.

## Theory B: the Dawnfaith's Severance (PROPOSED)

**Claim:** The world has become estranged from a sacred order. Industry, and the belief that life can be reduced
to calculation, broke humanity's bond with a real supernatural force. Renewal needs restoration and sacrifice, not
repair.

**Why people believe it:** Some sanctuaries still produce undeniable miracles. Rituals sometimes coincide with
local recovery. Places scarred by extraction feel spiritually dead.

**What it overlooks:** Correlation isn't divine judgement. Sacred sites fail too. Institutions exploit fear and
call dissent sacrilege. Some machines keep alive people prayer can't.

**Dissent inside:** Scholars such as Lira hold that the supernatural is real but priestly explanations still need
scrutiny. Healing orders reject punitive readings.

**Story seed:** A radiant sanctuary can save a nearby settlement, but the hierarchy demands an unacceptable
offering. Seraphine must tell her gift apart from the institution's demands.

## Theory C: the Lantern Cities' Stolen Light (PROPOSED)

**Claim:** The collapse looks universal because the powerful hoard the systems, resources and knowledge that
could ease it. The world may be failing, but the elites decide who survives.

**Why people believe it:** Merchant records show withheld shipments, engineered shortages, and rich districts
still lit while poor ones go dark. The Foundry and Dawnfaith impose embargoes and levies.

**What it overlooks:** Hoarding can make a real disaster worse without causing it. Some things genuinely can't be
replaced. The Cities' own brokers and gangs profit from scarcity.

**Dissent inside:** Mutual-aid networks against criminal monopolies; free-market brokers against those who want
essentials held in common.

**Story seed:** Jax finds proof a convoy was diverted from a starving district. But the missing cargo was also
meant to hold back an approaching threat.

## Shared clues (PROPOSED, each ambiguous)

| What is seen | Foundry reading | Dawnfaith reading | Lantern Cities reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| A tower loses power while a spell nearby keeps working | Separate failure domains; infrastructure breakdown | The spirit endures as machines fail | Someone diverted the fuel or parts |
| A revered ritual fails in a well-powered city | The supernatural is unstable | Grace lost, or a ritual corrupted by ambition | The authorities can't guarantee what they sell |
| An abandoned district keeps declining with nobody there | An unobserved process, system inertia | An old wound never healed | Abandonment itself was a political choice |
| A rescue succeeds using engineering and magic together | Complementary capabilities | Wisdom can live alongside machines | Cooperation beats ideology |

## How the mystery unfolds across games (PROPOSED)

- **Each game solves a human-scale mystery** completely: who diverted the medicine, why a fortress was abandoned,
  what a machine was ordered to do, why a ritual was faked.
- **Each game adds one piece of evidence** about the global decline, seen through its heroes' faction, with its
  bias stated.
- **No game invalidates another's evidence.** Later games can show the same event from a different side.
- **A game may resolve the global cause** only after an explicit creator decision and a full canon audit.

## Rules for revealing information

- A clue states **what was observed, who observed it, and how they might be biased**.
- A character's conviction is evidence of their worldview, not of the cosmos.
- No boss speech, codex page or AI announcement explains the ultimate cause by authorial fiat.

## The central irony

The heroes may stop people who are trying to *save the world*, because their way of saving it is doing harm now.
The tragedy is not that everyone is foolish, but that partial truths plus concentrated power make catastrophe.
