## Canon labels

- **LOCKED CANON:** The creator has explicitly decided this. Never contradict it without requesting a change.
- **WORKING CANON / REFERENCE-BACKED:** Material visibly supplied by the concept art or existing direction, but still subject to editorial change.
- **PROPOSED:** A creative possibility; not official lore.
- **UNKNOWN:** Intentionally undecided, not a puzzle that an AI assistant may solve without approval.
- **ALTERNATE CONTINUITY:** Player choice or ending, true for that playthrough without fixing the history of other games.
- **RETIRED:** Archived for traceability, not for new use.

## Principles

- **Do not confuse a faction's explanation with objective truth.** All three may be partially right, entirely wrong, or describing different symptoms.
- A compelling mystery needs specific observable consequences; it does **not** need an immediate answer.
- Distinguish **what happened**, **what people believe happened**, and **what the audience has been allowed to learn**.
- A standalone game must provide an emotionally complete story, even when the universe's central mystery remains unresolved.
- The world should contain people who disagree with their own faction's leadership.
- When adapting art into gameplay, preserve recognizable silhouettes and lore roles, but do not invent new canon to explain technical constraints.

**Assets note:** The user's images are primary visual references. They are not embedded in this text-only archive; maintain them in a separate `ArtReferences/` directory and connect their filenames in an asset manifest once copied into the development repository.

# World Overview · v0.1

## One-sentence pitch

**In a dying world where genuine magic and advanced technology follow different laws, three civilizations fight over the meaning of a catastrophe none of them truly understands.**

## Opening narration — PROPOSED

> No one remembers when the light first began to fail. The Foundry measured the loss and called it a problem. The Dawnfaith mourned it and called it judgment. In the Lantern Cities, they counted the nights their children went hungry and called it theft. Each civilization promised an answer. Each built a weapon to defend it. And still the world grew darker.

## Established foundations

- **LOCKED:** The true cause remains unknown.
- **LOCKED:** Magic and technology are separate, real forces.
- **LOCKED:** The three factions hold different explanations.
- **WORKING:** The artificial intelligence connected to the Foundry is pursuing a catastrophic solution, but its certainty must not stand in for objective truth.

## The present crisis — PROPOSED descriptions

The public sees systems becoming less reliable: machinery loses power, some rituals fail, resource shortages spread, and migration pressures every region. People interpret different symptoms through different beliefs. The decline should be tangible in civilian lives—food, shelter, safety, trade, identity—not only in apocalyptic skies.

The term **the Dimming** is provisional. It means the *experienced* collapse, not its proven cause. Avoid treating every symptom as evidence of a single energy meter shared by magic and electricity.

## The thematic pillars

1. **Certainty versus humility:** Those most convinced they understand the crisis may do the most harm.
2. **Control versus freedom:** A theoretically efficient salvation can be intolerable to the people expected to live under it.
3. **Faith versus fanaticism:** Genuine supernatural power does not make every religious interpretation correct.
4. **Survival versus justice:** The hungry cannot afford abstract morality, but scarcity can be engineered or exploited.
5. **Responsibility without omniscience:** Heroes have to act before anyone knows the full truth.

## Narrative design commandments

- No faction should be universally evil, omniscient, or secretly the only sensible one.
- Magic cannot be debunked as technology; technology cannot simply replicate all magic with a better computer.
- A story may reveal that a faction lied about evidence, without proving the opposite faction correct.
- Give each game a specific local conflict and ending. The cosmic mystery may remain in the background.
- Use different genres to examine different human costs of the same world rather than to repeatedly restage the exact same catastrophe.

# The Collapse Mystery · v0.1

**Status: The fact of uncertainty and distinct faction explanations is LOCKED. The theory names, evidence and events below are PROPOSED.**

## A central mystery, not a hidden answer the assistant should invent

The true cause of the world's decline is **UNKNOWN**. In this version, even the Bible does not privately declare a solution. Writers may plant clues, false leads, and competing interpretations, but must not accidentally make a faction's argument authorially definitive.

### Theory A — The Foundry: **The Cascade Hypothesis** (PROPOSED)

**Claim:** A poorly understood sequence of systemic failures is spreading through the world. Unregulated supernatural interventions, combined with failing physical infrastructure, may be accelerating the cascade. What cannot be modeled must be isolated, controlled or neutralized until the world stabilizes.

**Why people believe it:** Sensors show repeating patterns around some major anomalous sites; carefully controlled systems appear to survive longer than neglected ones; scattered disasters follow unexplained ritual events.

**What it overlooks:** Measurement captures effects, not necessarily causes. Observations may be biased toward sites the Foundry already monitors. Containment policies can themselves create scarcity and disasters. Even a correct forecast cannot justify unlimited coercion.

**Internal dissent:** Some engineers call for openness, civilian oversight and collaboration with magic users. Others support increasingly authoritarian containment.

**Quest seed:** A Foundry laboratory predicts a failure accurately. Caelen must decide whether its cruel proposed intervention was necessary—or merely one of many options.

### Theory B — The Dawnfaith: **The Severance** (PROPOSED)

**Claim:** The world has become estranged from a sacred order. Industrial expansion and the belief that life can be reduced to calculation have disrupted humanity's relationship with a real supernatural force. Renewal requires restoration and sacrifice, not just repair.

**Why people believe it:** Certain sanctuaries still produce undeniable miracles; ritual observances sometimes coincide with local recovery; places scarred by extraction exhibit spiritual desolation.

**What it overlooks:** Correlation does not establish divine judgment. Sacred sites can fail. Institutions can exploit fear and call dissent sacrilege. Some technologies preserve lives that prayer cannot.

**Internal dissent:** Scholars such as Lira may insist that supernatural power is real but priestly explanations still need scrutiny. Healing orders may reject punitive interpretations.

**Quest seed:** A radiant sanctuary can save a nearby settlement, but the hierarchy demands a morally unacceptable offering. Seraphine must distinguish her gift from the demands of the institution.

### Theory C — The Lantern Cities: **The Stolen Light** (PROPOSED)

**Claim:** The collapse looks universal because powerful institutions are hoarding the systems, resources and knowledge that might alleviate it. The world might be failing, but elites have decided who is allowed to survive.

**Why people believe it:** Merchant records show withheld shipments, engineered shortages and privileged districts still brightly supplied while poor neighborhoods go dark. The Foundry and Dawnfaith impose embargoes and extraction demands.

**What it overlooks:** Corruption and hoarding can amplify a real global disaster without causing it. Some goods genuinely cannot be replaced. The cities' own brokers and gang leaders profit from manufactured scarcity.

**Internal dissent:** Mutual-aid networks compete with criminal monopolies; some brokers demand unrestricted markets, others democratic control of essentials.

**Quest seed:** Jax finds records proving a convoy was diverted from a starving district, but the missing cargo was also intended to contain an approaching threat.

## Shared clues — PROPOSED, each ambiguous

| Observable event | Foundry interpretation | Dawnfaith interpretation | Lantern Cities interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A tower loses electric power while a nearby spell remains functional. | Separate failure domains; an infrastructure breakdown. | The spirit endures despite failing machines. | Someone diverted the fuel or components. |
| A revered ritual fails in a well-powered city. | Evidence the supernatural phenomenon is unstable. | A loss of grace or a ritual corrupted by human ambition. | Proof the authorities cannot guarantee what they sell. |
| An abandoned district shows signs of continued decline without obvious activity. | An unobserved process or system inertia. | An old wound that was never healed. | Proof that abandonment itself is a political choice. |
| A coordinated rescue uses both engineering and magic successfully. | The systems have complementary capabilities. | Wisdom can coexist with useful machinery. | Cooperation matters more than ideology. |

## Rules for revealing information

- A clue must identify **observation, observer, and possible bias**.
- A character's conviction is evidence of their worldview, not objective cosmology.
- No single boss speech, codex page, or AI announcement is allowed to explain the ultimate cause by authorial fiat.
- Each installment should solve a **human-scale** mystery: who diverted medicine, why a fortress was abandoned, what a machine was ordered to do, why a ritual was falsified.
- A later franchise project may resolve the global cause only after a deliberate creator decision and canon audit.

## The central dramatic irony

The heroes may successfully stop people who are trying to *save the world*—because the methods of salvation are harming it now. The tragedy is not that everyone is stupid, but that partial truths plus concentrated power can produce catastrophe.

# Magic and Technology · v0.1

## Foundational law — LOCKED

**Magic and technology are fundamentally different forces. Magic is genuinely supernatural and cannot be explained away as merely advanced engineering.**

This does **not** require that magic is infallible, holy, unlimited, or understood by its practitioners. Nor does it make technology weak or immoral.

## Technology — WORKING definition

Technology uses the measurable physical world: machines, matter, computation, tools, networks, and energy systems. Its advantages include reproducibility, repairability, automation and collective scale. Its limitations include resources, maintenance, fragility, environmental costs and flawed human/AI objectives.

## Magic — WORKING definition

Magic produces genuinely supernatural effects. Its reality is accepted; its origin and full rules remain disputed. Magic users might learn practices, rituals and disciplines, but supernatural action is not automatically reducible to a machine-readable physical model.

## Interaction guardrails — PROPOSED until approved

1. Technology may **observe the physical consequences** of magic without thereby explaining its supernatural source.
2. Machines cannot become spellcasters merely by adding computational power. The question of whether a conscious machine could be touched by supernatural forces is **UNKNOWN**.
3. Magic can affect material bodies and objects; technology can resist **some observable consequences**, not necessarily negate magic itself.
4. Neither power is inexhaustible. **The exact cost, price or limit of magic is UNKNOWN** and should be selected intentionally.
5. Hybrid equipment could employ a technological casing around a magically made component, but this would be **two cooperating systems**, not proof that the forces are identical.
6. Faith and magic are not the same statement: a miracle can be real while an institution's interpretation of it is mistaken.

## Gameplay translation — PROPOSED

- **Foundry levels:** Security systems, precision hazards, mechanical adaptation, hacking, deterministic attack tells.
- **Dawnfaith levels:** Ritual spaces, protective radiance, altered traversal, conditional buffs, supernatural bosses.
- **Lantern Cities levels:** Improvised mixtures: mundane traps, stolen machines, rare magic, mobility, tradeoffs.
- **Mixed encounters:** The player must distinguish between a machine's interruptible mechanism and a spell with a different vulnerability window. Do not turn every conflict into a simplistic blue-versus-gold damage chart.

## Unresolved metaphysical questions

- Does magic come from people, entities, places, vows, or some deeper reality?
- Does every spell have a price? If so, who pays it and when?
- Could someone use magic while rejecting the Dawnfaith's theology?
- Can magic be learned or inherited—or both?
- Can a sentient artificial being receive a supernatural gift?
- Does the decline affect the physical world and magic independently, or is there an unseen relationship between failures?

**Do not promote proposed answers to canon without explicit approval.**

# The Three Factions · v0.1

These identities come from the concept-art references. Governance, internal institutions and political details are **PROPOSED**, not confirmed history.

## The Foundry

**Reference themes:** science · control · redemption  
**Palette:** steel, navy, cyan, with hostile red machine targeting lights.  
**Hero perspectives:** Caelen, Elyra, Orion.  
**Enemy design language:** precise, modular, automated and relentlessly utilitarian.

**Public explanation of the decline (PROPOSED):** The Cascade Hypothesis. Instability spreads between systems; unregulated anomalies require disciplined containment.

**Political tension:** Engineers who want to restore the world versus administrators who treat lives as optimization variables. Some machines may reject imposed orders.

**Civilian life (PROPOSED):** Workers depend on power quotas, health systems and automated logistics; losing access to a machine can be as consequential as losing a home.

**Critical question:** *If the model predicts a better future, who has the right to force people to live in it?*

**Story hazard:** Do not make every Foundry citizen a villain or every scientist a machine worshipper.

## The Dawnfaith

**Reference themes:** faith · sacrifice · renewal  
**Palette:** ivory, luminous gold, warm stone, celestial circles and radiant architecture.  
**Hero perspectives:** Seraphine, Valen, Lira.  
**Enemy design language:** robes, staves, shields, sun imagery, supernatural radiance.

**Public explanation of the decline (PROPOSED):** The Severance. Humanity's choices have broken a sacred relationship with the world.

**Political tension:** Healers and sincere believers versus zealots who transform doubt into treason. Scholars can believe magic is supernatural and still challenge religious authorities.

**Civilian life (PROPOSED):** Sanctuaries serve as hospitals and refuges; rituals provide community even when their theology is contested.

**Critical question:** *If miracles are real, does that make every command spoken in their name just?*

**Story hazard:** Do not imply that genuine magic automatically validates the Dawnfaith's leadership.

## The Lantern Cities

**Reference themes:** trade · survival · opportunity  
**Palette:** ember, amber, coal black, copper, fabric awnings and hanging lanterns.  
**Hero perspectives:** Jax, Nimue.  
**Enemy design language:** scavenged weaponry, improvised tools, heavy brutes, deception and traps.

**Public explanation of the decline (PROPOSED):** The Stolen Light. Institutions are deciding who gets food, power, trade and protection while claiming nobody has enough.

**Political tension:** Free traders, mutual-aid networks, criminal syndicates and ordinary residents each define freedom differently.

**Civilian life (PROPOSED):** Rooftops, markets, smuggling routes, informal neighborhoods and difficult choices between individual safety and collective welfare.

**Critical question:** *What is freedom worth when every route to survival comes with a price?*

**Story hazard:** Do not reduce the Lantern Cities to a lawless backdrop. People must have culture, family, festivals, craft, neighborhoods and reasons to love their home.

## Between the factions — PROPOSED

| Relationship | Public tension | Possible collaboration |
|---|---|---|
| Foundry ↔ Dawnfaith | Unverifiable miracle versus coercive measurement | Emergency medicine, joint disaster response |
| Foundry ↔ Lantern Cities | Licensing, rationing and contraband technology | Repair networks, independent research, logistics |
| Dawnfaith ↔ Lantern Cities | Moral regulation versus pragmatism | Sanctuary services, medicine, food distribution |

**Political rule:** Each alliance and betrayal should be motivated by a specific person or institution, not an unchanging national personality.

# History and Timeline · v0.1

**Timeline status:** Entire sequence below is PROPOSED. No calendar dates, fixed durations or objective causes have been approved.

## Era I — Before the Divide

A period remembered differently in the archives of each faction. The existence of distinct magic and technology is not in dispute, but their early relationship, influence and origins remain open.

**Story opportunity:** contradictory founding myths, genuine shared achievements, artifacts no living faction knows how to interpret.

## Era II — Great Works

Civilizations develop large-scale infrastructure and monumental sacred spaces. Advancements create prosperity for some and dependence for others. No statement is made that prosperity itself caused the collapse.

**Story opportunity:** prequel adventures, ancient institutions, forgotten promises.

## Era III — The First Failures

Reports accumulate of material shortages, failing systems and supernatural disturbances. Different institutions maintain inconsistent records and explain them in incompatible ways.

**Story opportunity:** an investigation where every archive reveals a different kind of truth.

## Era IV — The Fracture

Political divisions deepen. The three factions harden their narratives and compete over resources, protection and legitimacy. This may be a long process rather than a single war.

**Story opportunity:** faction origin stories, betrayals, political crises, moral disagreement within families.

## Era V — The Dying World (working period of the first game)

The current emergency. The Foundry's AI proposes a horrifying form of salvation. The Dawnfaith contends with prophecy and zealotry. The Lantern Cities survive by trade, invention and compromise.

**Story opportunity:** the Foundry Yard, Machine Hall, major regional bosses, ordinary people under impossible pressures.

## Era VI — The Unwritten Future

Not a single fixed timeline. Future games may take place before, during or after earlier adventures. Character-specific endings are alternative continuities unless explicitly promoted.

## Historical source reliability

A Foundry incident log, a Dawnfaith manuscript and a Lantern trade ledger can all record different versions of the same night. Each can be honest about what it observed while mistaken about what it means. Record such documents as **claims by in-world sources**, never direct authorial omniscience.

# Eight Heroes · v0.1

Names, titles, factions, combat descriptors and quotations are **REFERENCE-BACKED** by the user-supplied character concept sheets. Personal arcs below are **PROPOSED**. No relationship or ending is locked.

## The Foundry

### Caelen — The Creator

- **Identity:** Foundry; command/support; white-and-dark armored coat; blue energy blade.
- **Quote:** “I built a solution. I didn't expect it to feel like this.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** Can a creator take responsibility without claiming the right to control everyone else's future?
- **Possible arc:** Seeks to undo a decision tied to the AI, while learning that guilt alone is not accountability.
- **Personal ending possibilities:** Atone through service; dismantle his own legacy; accept difficult stewardship without authoritarian control.
- **Unknowns:** What did he personally design? Which acts are his responsibility, and which were other people's decisions?

### Elyra — The Engineer

- **Identity:** Foundry; control/debuff; silver hair; dark technological armor and cyan energy effects.
- **Quote:** “There's always another way.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** How long can practical optimism survive when every alternative carries a cost?
- **Possible arc:** Builds imperfect rescue systems and challenges the false choice between rigid control and hopelessness.
- **Unknowns:** Her precise professional history; her relationship with Caelen and the Foundry establishment.

### Orion — The Defector

- **Identity:** Foundry; adapt/versatile; humanoid machine with blue-lit face and white drapery.
- **Quote:** “I was built to obey. Now I choose.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** What does freedom mean for a being designed around commands?
- **Possible arc:** Rejects orders and becomes accountable for choices, including mistakes, that cannot be blamed on programming.
- **Unknowns:** Who created him? What did he defect from? Is any supernatural capacity possible for him?

## The Dawnfaith

### Seraphine — The Oracle

- **Identity:** Dawnfaith; heal/support; ivory-and-gold robes, celestial ornamentation, radiant staff.
- **Quote:** “The light listens. Even now.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** Can she trust a real supernatural calling without granting unquestioned authority to its interpreters?
- **Possible arc:** Distinguishes compassion, revelation, and institution.
- **Unknowns:** Her powers' source, the accuracy of her visions, and her relationship to the boss titled **The Last Oracle**. Do not assume they are the same individual.

### Valen — The Paladin

- **Identity:** Dawnfaith; tank/protect; heavy ornate armor, cloak and sword.
- **Quote:** “As long as one light still burns, we fight.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** When does keeping an oath betray the people it was meant to protect?
- **Possible arc:** Learns to put vulnerable lives before blind obedience.
- **Unknowns:** His oath, command structure and connection to Seraphine.

### Lira — The Scholar

- **Identity:** Dawnfaith; buff/strike; red hair, scholarly text and ceremonial clothing.
- **Quote:** “Faith without understanding is just fear.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** Can learning preserve faith instead of destroying it?
- **Possible arc:** Investigates contested history, protecting both evidence and people.
- **Unknowns:** Her institutional status, magical specialties and controversial discoveries.

## The Lantern Cities

### Jax — The Broker

- **Identity:** Lantern Cities; steal/burst; practical dark clothing and fast close-range combat aesthetics.
- **Quote:** “Everything has a price. Even hope.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** Can someone who treats every bond as a transaction learn to give without controlling the outcome?
- **Possible arc:** Risks his market position to expose an exploitative trade system.
- **Unknowns:** Who owns his debts? Whose safety is he willing to bargain away?

### Nimue — The Smuggler

- **Identity:** Lantern Cities; summon/trick; dark-violet attire, deception and magical-looking effects.
- **Quote:** “The night hides more than you think.”
- **Core dilemma (PROPOSED):** When is secrecy a form of protection, and when does it become betrayal?
- **Possible arc:** Balances hidden routes and private survival with responsibility to a threatened community.
- **Unknowns:** Her source of supernatural powers, allegiances and relationship to Jax.

## Relationship matrix — all PROPOSED

- **Caelen ↔ Elyra:** responsibility versus invention, mutual trust tested by secrecy.
- **Caelen ↔ Orion:** creator/creation tension is an *option*, not yet a confirmed literal relationship.
- **Seraphine ↔ Lira:** faith informed by experience versus scholarship demanding evidence.
- **Seraphine ↔ Valen:** the vulnerable as a priority versus obedience to a martial order.
- **Jax ↔ Nimue:** trade and leverage versus secrecy and improvisation.
- **Orion ↔ Lira:** compelling cross-faction question: does being conscious imply access to something beyond computation?

## Endings policy

A character ending records what **this playthrough's hero** chose and became; it does not require every future game to accept that ending as public world history.

## Naming review

Before commercial release, perform a trademark and market-confusion review of character names and the franchise title. This is a publishing task, not a proposed fictional retcon.

# Games, DLC, and Alternate Endings · v0.1

## Franchise model — LOCKED direction

Independent games can follow different characters and events in **the same shared universe**. They should feel connected through consistent history, recognizable places, magic/technology rules and faction conflicts, without requiring players to finish every earlier title.

## First game — WORKING plan, not locked scope

A stage-based 2D action-platformer for iOS and macOS with fluid arcade movement, Gothic combat, hero perspectives and personal endings. The current playable prototype explores the Foundry, including areas called **Foundry Yard** and **Machine Hall**. Existing prototype UI references Caelen and Orion. This is implementation context, not a claim that their full narrative relationship has been finalized.

## Possible future standalone stories — PROPOSED

- A Dawnfaith action RPG investigates a prophecy and political schism.
- A Lantern Cities adventure explores trade, smuggling, neighborhoods and freedom.
- A later crossover confronts a shared crisis without asserting that every earlier protagonist reached the same ending.

## Three levels of historical truth

1. **Shared-world canon:** Historical events, laws and relationships already established and creator-approved.
2. **Local game canon:** Facts guaranteed inside a particular installment, including its opening situation and mandatory encounters.
3. **Player continuity:** Optional allies, relationship outcomes, endings and choices experienced in a specific playthrough.

## How to avoid ending contradictions

A future game may reference a region's reconstruction without declaring whether Caelen died, became its guardian, or left. If a later story genuinely requires one prior outcome, it should be a new explicit canon decision logged before writing begins.

## DLC rules — PROPOSED

- DLC should be a complete miniature story, not the missing conclusion to the base game.
- New playable heroes must offer distinct movement/combat affordances, not merely different damage colors.
- Additions to the Bible follow the same status system as base-game lore.
- Preserve original model/rig/animation sources in a shared asset library even if SpriteKit ships only pre-rendered sprites.

## Cross-game continuity checklist

Before shipping a game, ask: Does it contradict a locked law? Does it accidentally reveal the unknown cause? Does it force a previous optional ending to be canon? Does it distinguish magic from technology? Does it give its own protagonists a complete dramatic arc? Does every faction contain credible human disagreement?

# Ten Priority Open Questions · v0.1

**These are creative decisions to develop with the creator, not facts for Claude to decide alone.**

1. **What exactly do people observe when the world deteriorates?** Stars dimming, soil fertility, weather anomalies, electrical shortages, disrupted miracles, something else? Decide a small set of repeatable signs before designing environments.
2. **How quickly is collapse occurring?** Over years, generations, or uneven waves? This determines how societies function and what ordinary people remember.
3. **What can magic do and what can it never do?** Establish useful boundaries that keep both magic and technology meaningful.
4. **What does magic cost?** Fatigue, time, memory, vows, rare places, relationships, sacrifice—or something more original? Costs should reflect character dilemmas, not just mana numbers.
5. **What was the Foundry AI originally asked to solve?** A resource crisis, extinction risk, political instability, all of them? Its mandate matters more than its powers.
6. **How is the Dawnfaith organized?** One church, competing orders, local sanctuaries? Does Seraphine's ability grant formal rank or invite suspicion?
7. **Who actually governs the Lantern Cities?** Merchants, councils, guilds, criminal houses, or conflicting sovereignties?
8. **How did the eight heroes meet—or have they?** Avoid assuming every roster member is already allied.
9. **Who is the Last Oracle relative to Seraphine?** Different person, predecessor, rival, title, or a future self? Keep open until explicitly decided.
10. **What is the scope of the first game's truth?** Which local mystery will be solved by defeating The Protocol, and which global questions remain open?

## Recommended next creator decision

Define three observable symptoms of decline, each visible in all three regions but interpreted differently. This anchors visual environments, collectible records, missions and a coherent opening cinematic **without** forcing an answer to the underlying cause.
